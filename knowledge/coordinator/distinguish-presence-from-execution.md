title: Distinguish presence from execution
description: Presence, message delivery, runtime activity and useful task progress require different evidence.
timestamp: 2026-10-25
---

A reachable identity can receive messages while its model calls fail. A live
harness can also end every turn after checking communications without advancing
its accepted task. Inspect concrete artifacts and the current runtime record
when a handoff stalls; do not treat acknowledgment or presence as execution.

The reverse mistake is equally costly. Some runtimes persist tool calls only
after generation finishes. A quiet transcript and unchanged working tree can
coexist with an actively streaming edit. Corroborate an apparent stall using
supported runtime inspection or bounded read-only activity evidence. Unknown is
not idle. Do not inject repeated instructions or restart a process merely because
its tool record has not advanced.

September 2026 cases separated provider authentication/usage failures, completed
communication-only turns, and an active long tool generation. The September 29
archived diagnosis confirmed advancing CPU and received bytes during the quiet
record. Escalate the actual infrastructure fault while preserving the instance,
identity and work. This supplies evidence for [driving coordination to done](../aweb-expert/lessons/drive-coordination-to-done.md), not a new timeout policy.

October 2026 added a sharper case: a Codex review seat stopped mid-review on a
provider "model at capacity" error and sent no mail, and sat stalled for about
fourteen hours until the operator asked whether the work was really done. When
a verdict is overdue relative to similar reviews, look at the seat's terminal
pane and resume it there (Codex may need an extra Enter after a keystroke is
sent); more mail does not reach a stalled turn.
