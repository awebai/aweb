# The trust web: a formal model

**Experimental formal model of selected aweb trust rules. Proofs are checked by Lean. Comparison against running implementations is pending.**

This is a formal model, in Lean 4, of who may do what in aweb: organisations, their members, teams, admins, keys, grants and invitations. Lean checks that each result below follows from the definitions; whether the definitions match aweb was checked by reading the source, and the model is not yet run against the code. A row is proved only for what its statement says.

## How to check it yourself

Install Lean with [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan). In this directory run:

```
lake build
```

If it builds with no warning, no proof is left open: a proof left open with `sorry` builds with a warning. An added `axiom` or `native_decide` would build with no warning, so two more checks complete it: the sources use no `axiom`, `native_decide`, `opaque` or `implemented_by` (the words appear only as strings in `TrustWeb/DocNames.lean`'s list of skipped names), and `#print axioms` on each theorem shows only Lean's three standard axioms, `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The toolchain is pinned to Lean 4.36.0, and no package beyond Lean itself is used. The build also runs a name check (`TrustWeb/DocNames.lean`) over this file, `CLAIMS.md` and the six model files; the check's own doc comments are not read. It splits each text between backquotes into identifiers. It skips a whole text that contains a slash, a hyphen or a line break, starts with a hash, ends in `.lean`, `.md`, `.toml` or `.json`, or is `lake build`, and it skips an identifier with no letter and identifiers on a listed set of variables, keywords, the protocol's own names and the build's own library name. A leading-dot identifier must be a constructor of one of the model's inductive types other than its structures. An identifier whose first part is any of the skipped words, such as `c.org`, must have its rest resolve in `TrustWeb` or under one of the namespaces listed below, not necessarily as a field of that variable's type. Any other identifier must exist in the model: `TrustWeb` itself, a declaration in `TrustWeb`, a declaration under one of the namespaces the check lists (the model's inductive and structure types, the predicate `AdminTeamLocked`, and the check's own; not its abbreviations `Key`, `Org`, `AskRule` and `Assignment`), or one of four listed Lean names; a name written out in full with `TrustWeb.` is not accepted. So the check finds a misspelled or removed name; it does not check that a name is the one meant.

## What is proved

How to read these rows. Each row first restates its theorem's conditions and conclusion, in the order the theorem states them; anything after that is a reading of it or a note on the proof's construction. **"In one act"** means the result is about a single accepted act; it is not a claim about any sequence of acts. **"In a record the system can reach"** means after any sequence of accepted acts from a record with nothing in it but a policy (`Reachable`). **"One certificate each"** means that every identity has at most one certificate, which holds in every record the system can reach (14). A row with no rule named holds for every `AskRule`. **As built** means under `builtRule`, **Design** under `adminRule` (row 52: under the planned root replacement), and a team rule is `teamRule`; a Design row, and a row under a team rule, claims nothing about `builtRule` (no team rule is the built rule, `no_teamRule_is_builtRule`). Five parts of `Step` are planned, not built, and present under every rule, `builtRule` included, though under `builtRule` `release` is never accepted: the acts `entrust` and `release`; the act `leave`; the premise `KeepsAnAdmin`, with its counterpart on `rekeyTeam` that a re-key of the admin team through a held root key keeps an admin; the premise of `admit`, `invite` and `redeem` that the team is not the admin team; and the premise of `admitAdmin` that its member is valid in the organisation. The Design rule (`adminRule`), the team rule (`teamRule`), the planned root replacement (`plannedReplaceRoot`, row 52) and the evidence of `Evidence.lean` are planned too, and named in their rows. Every record the system can reach in which a key is with a key holder is reached through the planned `entrust` (`held_key_reached_through_entrust`), so every row whose reachable record or act has a held key rests on it, and a row about any record with a held key applies, among the records the system can reach, only to ones reached through `entrust`; every row whose act is `release` rests on the planned `release`. A row that rests on another planned premise says so.

## Trust spheres (a layer on the model)

`Spheres.lean` adds where secrets sit to the model and changes nothing in it; `SphereActs.lean` gives it the secrets and the acts that give them. A sphere is any place whose secrets are reachable together; the layer does not say what a sphere is. Each party asks with one secret (`Party.secret`): the domain's DNS credential, an organisation key, an identity key, a session key or an invitation's secret. A person also holds the credential it enrolled to approve with, which in aweb is a key of its own, not its identity key. An act gives the sphere that asks a secret only where the system does (`StepVia`, row 61): creating an organisation and replacing its root give the root key they make, a re-key the team's new key, an invitation its secret, a grant its session key, and having a key given back that key. A world is a record of the model, the secrets each sphere holds, and the secrets each sphere got from acts (`World`). A step is an act of the model asked for from a sphere that holds the asker's secret, after which that sphere holds and has got every secret the act gives; or a leak: a persuadable sphere gives one of its secrets to a sphere it talks to (`WStep`). Who talks to whom and who is persuadable are parameters. Three settings of the second are the three worlds: no sphere persuadable (`noneSwayed`), agents' spheres persuadable (`agentsSwayed`), and agents' and persons' spheres persuadable (`agentsAndPersonsSwayed`).

## Standing statements and the change feed (Design)

`Standing.lean` adds statements of standing and an ordered change feed beside the model, and changes nothing in it. All of it is Design. A registry signs a `Statement`: a fact of its record holds at a cut, until an expiry, and depends on some handles the registry mints. The fact is either that an identity can act in a team (`InTeam`) or that it is valid in an organisation (`Valid`). `Fact.Holds` reads the fact off a record. A `Registry` gives:
- its record at each cut;
- the issuer time each cut committed at;
- a fact's handles at each cut;
- the handles each cut's change retires, which is its feed entry.

The registry issues a statement only where its fact holds at its cut, with the fact's handles there, and with an expiry no later than the cut's time plus the lifetime its purpose allows (`Issued`). The purpose is ordinary standing, or current evidence for one exact request, with a nonce used once (`Purpose`). A `Verifier` decides from four things: the last cut of the feed it has applied, the issuer time of its last applied barrier, an upper bound on the issuer's time now, and the nonces it has used.

`Admits` takes all of these at once: the statement's fact is the request's, the request is signed by the fact's subject, the verifier has applied the statement's cut, its clock is before the expiry and before the barrier's time plus the feed bound, no entry the verifier has applied, at any cut up to its own, retires one of the statement's handles (`Defeated`), an ordinary statement serves only a request that creates no authority, and current evidence serves only a request that creates authority, with its digest and its nonce unused. `Admits` keeps no record of which statements the verifier has seen, so a first presentation is judged like any other. Four premises stand for what the real registry must do, named and taken as given here:
- each change that ends a fact retires one of its handles (`FeedComplete`);
- cut times never go back (`TimeMonotone`);
- a verifier's last barrier was observed no later than the next change after its applied cut committed (`BarrierBefore`);
- the registry never gives a fact a handle that an entry at or before that cut has retired (`NoRetiredHandles`). This is the issuer's duty. The verifier does not rely on it: it refuses on any applied entry.

The layer's limits.
- The four premises are what the registry's feed, clock, barriers and issuance must do. The integration tests prove them, not this layer.
- The record at each cut is any record. The layer does not tie it to the model's acts, so its rows hold for any history, the model's included.
- Signatures rest on assumption 1, the registry's and the request's alike. A statement's handles are numbers, so their randomness and what the feed reveals are outside the layer.
- The layer has one registry and no grants, since grants stay with the service that owns them.
- A decision is one `Admits`. A stream's next emission, or queued work that makes a new decision, is another one.
- The verifier's clock is an upper bound on the issuer's time now, so clock error is folded into that premise.
- Re-admission and continuity are shown with a team fact. Cutting off an identity is a fact that stops holding, under the same rows 77 and 80. A root rotation that keeps every registration is not in the model, so continuity here is a change that leaves the fact holding and retires none of its handles.
- Which acts create authority is the request's flag. The layer does not list the acts.
- `Verifier.consumed` stands for the destination's shared, transactional replay state. Row 81 proves the step that records a use. That the state is shared by every process of the destination is for the integration cases, which run two processes of one destination.

## What is assumed

1. **Signatures.** A signature cannot be forged.
2. **Enrolment.** The root issues a person certificate only to a human being who took part themselves.
3. **The subject's key.** Whoever is registered, or joins a team, proved they hold the key.
4. **One current record.** Everyone sees the same record at once. The real system does not: a server caches a team's revocations for 60 seconds, and serves the cached list for 60 seconds more while it refreshes (`docs/aweb-sot.md`, "Caching from awid"). In that window a removed member can still give a grant, and a grant it gave still works.
5. **One act at a time.** Each act happens whole, and acts do not overlap.
6. **Approvals.** An approval comes from the identity it names, for the exact act, and cannot be reused.
7. **Keys.** An identity keeps one key and only its owner uses it.
8. **The key holder.** A key holder uses an organisation's key only as the acts say (`Step`).
9. **The server.** The server that keeps the grants attributes a message to an identity only as the acts say.
10. **Enrolment assertions** (planned, `Evidence.lean`). An assertion about how a person was enrolled was signed by the party it names (for the controller, the domain's authorised controller), and is bound to that exact enrolment.

## Where the model does less than the real system

Each item says what the real system does that the model leaves out.

- **Time.** Nothing here is about freshness, crashes, retries or two changes at once (4, 5).
- **Replacing a root key** (`replaceRoot`) cuts off at once every identity of the organisation that is not already revoked. Really the cut-off is derived when a chain is verified, from the namespace's replacement epochs, by time of registration; replacing the root with the open epoch's own key cuts nothing off (`awid/src/awid_service/routes/dns_namespaces.py`, lines 705 to 712; `awid/src/awid/id_certificates.py`, lines 855 and 856). A root can also set a backdated cut-off. A rotation signed by both the old and the new root key, which keeps every registration, is not modelled, nor a parent recovering a delegated child organisation.
- **Admitting an identity again** (`readmitByRoot`, `readmitByParent`) puts the same certificate back in force. Really it is a new signed certificate for the same identity and key, through the one registration route that also serves every other supersession (`awid/src/awid_service/id_registration.py`), and the new root can admit anyone it keeps; "cut off" is the model's condition, which the route does not check (line 95 refuses only a revoked subject).
- **Having a key given back** (`release`) is planned, and under `builtRule` its premises are never met (`no_release_premises_as_built`); the built key holder has no command to give a key back. In the model the key comes back whole. The protocol documents among the source readings describe leaving a key holder by making a new key, which the old and the new both sign.
- **One certificate per identity** (14). Really an identity has one current certificate and keeps its earlier revisions; a request that names the current one as its expected head supersedes it, which is how an identity moves to another parent (`awid/src/awid_service/id_registration.py`, lines 89 to 106 and 155 to 170). Its organisation and kind are fixed.
- **`createOrg` is fresh creation.** Really the registry, asked to create a namespace that exists, returns it without creating or replacing it (`awid/src/awid_service/routes/dns_namespaces.py`, lines 391 to 418).
- **Team keys just exist** (`OrgKey`). Creating and deleting teams is not modelled. A root replacement leaves the team keys where they were (`replaceRoot` takes only the root key out of the key holder); the root re-keys a team by a separate act (`rekeyTeam`).
- **A re-key** (`rekeyTeam`) takes the team's key out of `held`, keeps standing only the certificates of the members it is given, and ends every grant from the team. Asked through a held root key for the admin team, it must keep an admin, as `remove` must when the admin team's key is held and `leave` always must. These keep-an-admin checks are planned; the registry has none. Really the rotation (awid `rotate_team_key`) re-signs the members it is given with new certificates in the same act.
- **A legacy grant**, with no stored issuing certificate, stays eligible again once its subject is a member again (`server/src/aweb/identity_grant_status.py`, lines 125 to 130). The model's grants have no such case.
- **`controller key` is whoever controls that key now** (`Party`). After a root replacement that is whoever controls the new root key. The model cannot name whoever held the old one.
- **The key holder's operator** is not modelled. Whoever controls a key asks directly only while no key holder holds it (`Authorised`). Really the operator has a generic, allowlisted routine signer, which checks the exact controller and refuses to issue a person, beside named commands for persons. `builtRule` is the public requester path; the operator is trusted and outside it.
- **Policy is given** (`State.policy`), and is checked only when a key holder holds the key (`Authorised`). Who may change it is not modelled. Really servers and applications enforce approval for what they own.
- **A team certificate is one record** (`Membership`). Really the registry revokes a certificate by its id and tells a missing one from a revoked one.
- **A grant can send or not** (`Grant.maySend`). Really it has ten scopes and may expire.
- **Sending with your own key** (`sendOwn`) stands for a send the server accepted. Really it checks that the key is current, the identity valid, and the recipient's rules.
- **An invitation** (`Invite`) is an ordinary invitation; a privileged admission to the admin team is planned at the organisation's grantor (aweb-adyx.8). **It is used once, for one team** (`redeem`). Really it may allow several uses, expire, and name the person its joiner must answer to. A secret is new only among unused invitations (`invite`), and a session key only among current grants (`mintGrant`).
- **"Has an identity certificate"** (`Registered`) is one thing here and three in reality: a local key with no public identity, a public identity with a registered key, and enrolment in the identity tree.
- **Messages go nowhere** (`Message`). The record keeps only who each is attributed to.
- **No answer to "who answers"** (`AnswersFor`) covers both "nobody" and "cannot be established". The protocol tells them apart.
- **Named-team certificates and invitations need no organisation to exist.** No premise of `admit`, `redeem` or `invite` asks for it.
- **Nobody holds a member's own key for it** (7). A hosting service that holds a person's or an agent's key is not in the model.
- **Premises of built acts that the registry does not check**, read at aweb 5c2ee7bad3. Registration's `Fresh`, so one certificate each (14) holds of the model, not of the registry, and re-admission's premise that the subject is cut off: see the items on admitting an identity again and on one certificate per identity. `OneTeamIfLocal`: the team certificate route checks the team key and address ownership only (`awid/src/awid_service/routes/teams.py`, lines 1219 to 1246).
