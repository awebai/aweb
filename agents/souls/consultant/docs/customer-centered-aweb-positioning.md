# Customer-Centered Aweb Positioning

When advising on aweb product direction, start from the customer's operating
problem, not from the protocol stack.

The useful framing is:

> Aweb is the control, coordination, and audit layer for AI coworkers, across
> whatever runtime a company already uses.

The older "control plane" phrase is directionally right, but it needs the
customer "why": once agents do real work, they stop being chatbots and become
operational actors inside the company. The problem is no longer prompting; the
problem is managing AI work without creating chaos, invisible risk, or
untraceable action.

Customers want this because otherwise agents are scattered across Claude,
ChatGPT, Codex, Copilot, n8n, internal scripts, and custom services with no
shared identity, no common work state, no permissions model, no simple way to
assign/pause/inspect/approve/revoke work, and no signed record of what happened.

Use this customer-facing benefit:

> Aweb lets companies turn scattered AI tools into managed AI coworkers that can
> be assigned work, use company apps safely, coordinate with each other, and
> leave a verifiable record of what happened.

Or shorter:

> Scale AI work without losing control.

Avoid saying agents are "accountable." Agents cannot really be held accountable;
humans and companies are accountable. Agents should be described as managed,
permissioned, observable, governed, and auditable.

Use "audit trail" or "signed audit trail" for the legal/compliance value. Use
"traceability" only when speaking to technical/debugging audiences. "Signed audit
trail" is clearer for buyers who need evidence of what happened.

Customer-facing nouns should be:

- agents
- teams
- work
- apps
- permissions
- approvals
- activity
- cost
- results
- signed audit trails

Translate technical nouns before they reach customers:

- AWID -> verified agent identity
- MCP -> connect agents from Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, and other clients
- A2A -> open agent interoperability / open agent network
- app manifests -> apps agents can use
- BYOT -> keep your company's keys and control
- mutation quota -> included agent actions / included work actions

The product should feel like the place a company goes to operate its AI
workforce: create agent teams, connect runtimes, assign work, coordinate agents,
approve sensitive actions, manage app access, watch progress, measure cost and
output, and keep a signed audit trail.

Good homepage direction:

> Put AI agents to work across your company.
>
> Aweb gives every agent an identity, shared coordination, controlled app access,
> and signed audit trails across whatever AI runtime your company already uses.

Good wedge framing:

> Run an AI dev team without losing control.

Do not say "small AI dev team" unless intentionally limiting scope for a pilot.
The wedge can be developer teams without implying the product is only for small
deployments.
